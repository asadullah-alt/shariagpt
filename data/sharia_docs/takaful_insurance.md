# Takaful — Islamic Insurance

## Definition
Takaful (Arabic: تكافل, "guaranteeing each other") is the Islamic alternative to conventional
insurance. It operates on the principle of mutual cooperation and shared responsibility, where
participants contribute to a common fund used to cover losses suffered by any member of the group.

## Why Conventional Insurance Is Problematic
Conventional insurance contracts contain three elements prohibited in Islamic law:
1. **Riba (interest)**: Premiums are invested in interest-bearing instruments.
2. **Gharar (excessive uncertainty)**: The insured pays premiums but may receive nothing, or may
   receive far more than paid — the outcome is fundamentally uncertain.
3. **Maysir (gambling)**: The speculative nature resembles gambling where one party gains at the
   expense of another.

## How Takaful Works
1. **Participants** contribute donations (tabarru') into a Takaful fund.
2. The **Takaful Operator** manages the fund and investments (in Sharia-compliant assets only).
3. Claims are paid from the fund; surplus is returned to participants or carried forward.
4. The operator earns a fee (Wakalah) or a share of profit (Mudaraba) — not the premium itself.

## Takaful Operator Models

### Wakalah Model (Agency)
- Participants pay into a risk fund.
- Operator charges a fixed fee (wakalah fee) for management services.
- Any underwriting surplus belongs to participants.
- Most common in the Middle East and Southeast Asia.

### Mudaraba Model (Profit-Sharing)
- Operator earns a share of the investment returns on the Takaful fund.
- Less common today; AAOIFI recommends Wakalah or hybrid.

### Hybrid Model (Wakalah + Mudaraba)
- Operator receives wakalah fee for underwriting management.
- Operator also receives share of investment surplus.

## Types of Takaful Products
- **Family Takaful**: Life and savings (equivalent of life insurance + endowment).
- **General Takaful**: Motor, property, health (equivalent of general insurance).
- **Retakaful**: Islamic reinsurance.

## Mal Takaful Products
- Motor Takaful: comprehensive and third-party coverage
- Property Takaful: mandatory for Ijara and Musharaka home finance
- Medical Takaful: individual and group plans
- All contributions invested in Sharia-certified assets (no conventional bonds or equities)
- Annual surplus distribution to eligible participants
