# Murabaha Financing — Sharia Rules & Product Overview

## Definition
Murabaha is a cost-plus-profit sale contract that is one of the most widely used Islamic financing
structures. The financier (bank or Islamic institution) purchases an asset at cost price and then
sells it to the customer at a disclosed markup (profit margin). The total price and profit are agreed
upfront, making the transaction transparent and Sharia-compliant.

## Key Sharia Conditions
1. **Asset must exist**: The financier must first own the asset before selling it. Selling something
   not yet owned (bay al-ma'dum) is prohibited.
2. **Disclosure of cost**: The original purchase price and the profit margin must be clearly disclosed
   to the buyer.
3. **Fixed price**: Once agreed, the sale price cannot be changed. No floating or variable profit
   rates are permitted in a classical Murabaha.
4. **No interest (riba)**: The profit earned is from a legitimate trade transaction, not a loan with
   interest. The difference between the purchase price and sale price is the halal profit.
5. **Risk transfer**: The financier must bear risk of ownership for at least a moment before selling.

## Deferred Payment (Murabaha to the Purchase Orderer)
In modern Islamic banking, the most common form is "Murabaha to the Purchase Orderer" (MPO):
- Customer identifies the asset they need.
- Customer promises (wa'd) to buy it from the bank once the bank acquires it.
- Bank buys the asset and sells it to the customer at cost + disclosed profit.
- Customer pays in installments (deferred payment / bay bithaman ajil).

## Permissible Uses
- Home equipment and appliances financing
- Vehicle financing
- Trade finance (commodity Murabaha)
- Small business equipment

## Prohibited Elements
- The bank cannot charge additional profit if the customer is late (this would be riba).
- A penalty for late payment may only be donated to charity, not retained by the bank.
- The bank cannot sell the asset back to the original seller in a back-to-back transaction
  (this would constitute tawarruq or organized Murabaha, which some scholars prohibit).

## Mal Murabaha Product Features
- Profit rate: Fixed, disclosed at contract signing
- Tenure: 12 to 84 months
- Eligible assets: vehicles, electronics, home appliances, equipment
- Early settlement: allowed with potential rebate (ibra) at bank's discretion
