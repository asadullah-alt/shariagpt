# Ijara — Islamic Leasing: Rules & Structure

## Definition
Ijara (Arabic: إجارة) means "to give something on rent." It is an Islamic leasing contract in which
the lessor (owner) transfers the right to use an asset to the lessee for a specified period in
exchange for periodic rental payments. Ownership remains with the lessor throughout the lease term.

## Sharia Distinction: Ownership vs. Usufruct
- **Ownership** (raqaba): Belongs to the lessor (bank/financier) throughout the contract.
- **Usufruct** (manfa'ah): The right to use and benefit from the asset, transferred to lessee.
- Rental is paid for the usufruct only — not for the asset itself.

## Key Sharia Conditions
1. **Asset must exist and be deliverable** at the time the contract is executed.
2. **Asset must be usable without consumption** — Ijara is not valid for consumable goods.
3. **Rent must be clearly defined**: amount, currency, and payment schedule must be fixed at signing.
4. **Maintenance**: Major structural maintenance is the lessor's responsibility (cost is Sharia
   obligation of ownership). Minor operational maintenance is the lessee's responsibility.
5. **Risk of asset**: If the asset is destroyed through no fault of the lessee, the lessor bears
   the loss. The lessee does not owe rent for periods the asset is unusable.

## Ijara wa Iqtina (Lease-to-Own / Hire Purchase)
The most common Islamic home finance structure:
- Bank purchases property and leases it to customer.
- Customer pays monthly rental.
- A separate promise (wa'd) allows customer to purchase at a future date.
- Ownership transfers via a separate sale contract (not within the Ijara itself).
- Customer's monthly payment is split: rental portion + ownership acquisition portion.

## Diminishing Musharaka + Ijara (Home Finance)
Often combined:
- Bank and customer co-own the property.
- Customer leases the bank's share.
- Customer gradually purchases the bank's units of ownership.
- Rent decreases as customer's ownership share increases.

## Mal Ijara Home Finance Features
- Property types: residential villas, apartments (UAE)
- Maximum finance amount: AED 5,000,000
- Tenure: up to 25 years
- Profit rate: fixed or variable (linked to benchmark, with Sharia-compliant cap)
- Takaful (Islamic insurance) for property is mandatory
- No penalty for early termination; rebate (ibra) may apply
