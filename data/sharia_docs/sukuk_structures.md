# Sukuk — Islamic Bonds: Structures & Sharia Principles

## Definition
Sukuk (singular: sakk) are Sharia-compliant financial certificates that represent proportional
ownership in an underlying tangible asset, usufruct, or project. Unlike conventional bonds, sukuk
do not represent a debt obligation with interest. Instead, investors hold a proportional share in
real assets and receive income derived from those assets.

## Why Conventional Bonds Are Prohibited
Conventional bonds pay interest (riba), which is explicitly prohibited in the Quran (2:275–280).
Sukuk circumvent this by structuring returns as profit from trade, rent from leasing, or profit
from a partnership — all of which are permissible under Islamic law.

## Common Sukuk Structures

### 1. Ijara Sukuk (Lease-Backed)
- Most common globally (70%+ of issuances).
- Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) buys assets and leases them back to the originator.
- Investors receive periodic rental income (lease payments).
- Asset ownership reverts at maturity.

### 2. Musharaka Sukuk (Partnership-Based)
- Investors hold equity stake in a joint venture.
- Returns are profit-sharing; losses are shared proportionally.
- Used for large infrastructure projects.

### 3. Mudaraba Sukuk (Profit-Sharing)
- Investors are rab al-mal (capital providers); issuer is the mudarib (manager).
- Returns depend on actual project profits.
- Capital is not guaranteed — investors bear loss risk.

### 4. Istisna Sukuk (Manufacturing/Construction)
- Linked to a manufacturing or construction contract.
- Payments made in stages as construction progresses.

## Sharia Requirements for Sukuk
- Must be backed by tangible, identifiable assets (not just a pool of receivables).
- Sukuk cannot guarantee capital repayment — any capital protection must come from a separate
  purchase undertaking (wa'd) structure.
- The underlying asset must be Sharia-permissible (no alcohol, gambling, weapons, etc.).
- AAOIFI Standard 17 governs sukuk structuring globally.
