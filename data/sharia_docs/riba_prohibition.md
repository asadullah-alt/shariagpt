# Riba, Gharar & Maysir — Core Prohibitions in Islamic Finance

## 1. Riba (Interest / Usury)

### Definition
Riba literally means "increase" or "excess." In Islamic law, it refers to any predetermined,
guaranteed excess charged on a loan or exchange contract beyond the principal.

### Quranic Basis
The prohibition is stated explicitly in the Quran:
- "Allah has permitted trade and forbidden riba" (Quran 2:275)
- "O you who believe, do not consume riba, doubled and multiplied" (Quran 3:130)

### Two Types of Riba
1. **Riba al-Nasi'ah** (Riba of deferment): Charging additional amount for extending the repayment
   period of a loan. This is the most common form — equivalent to conventional interest.
2. **Riba al-Fadl** (Riba of excess): Exchanging the same commodity in unequal quantities
   (e.g., 1kg gold for 1.1kg gold). Must exchange equal for equal, hand to hand.

### Implications for Islamic Banking
- Banks cannot charge interest on loans.
- Late payment fees cannot be retained by the bank — they must be donated to charity.
- Returns must come from legitimate trade, rent, or profit-sharing — not from lending money itself.

## 2. Gharar (Excessive Uncertainty)

### Definition
Gharar refers to uncertainty, ambiguity, or deception in a contract that could lead to dispute.
Not all uncertainty is prohibited — minor or unavoidable gharar is tolerated. Only excessive gharar
(gharar fahish) that materially affects the contract is prohibited.

### Examples of Prohibited Gharar
- Selling fish still in the sea (undeliverable object)
- Selling crops before they appear (uncertain existence)
- Unclear pricing or terms in a contract

### Implications
- All Islamic finance contracts must have clear: price, object, delivery terms, and payment schedule.
- Options and conventional derivatives contain gharar — special Sharia-compliant structures
  (wa'd — unilateral promises) are used instead.

## 3. Maysir (Gambling / Speculation)

### Definition
Maysir refers to games of chance or speculative transactions where one party gains at the direct
expense of another without productive economic activity.

### Why It Is Prohibited
- Quran 5:90 prohibits "intoxicants, gambling, stone altars, and divination arrows."
- Maysir creates wealth redistribution without real economic contribution.

### Finance Implications
- Highly speculative derivatives, short-selling, and certain structured products may be prohibited.
- Investments in casinos, lotteries, and gambling companies are haram.
- Islamic funds use strict screening to exclude maysir-related businesses.
